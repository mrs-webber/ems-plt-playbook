### Agenda

| Time | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Review team norms and roles, then revisit your essential standard, learning targets, and success criteria. |
| 5–20 min | Write anchors: each teacher writes what a proficient response to one key learning target looks like — and a not-yet-proficient one. |
| 20–33 min | Compare and find the line: put the anchors side by side and name where you disagree about whether a response is "there yet." |
| 33–42 min | Refine your success criteria so they capture what the team agreed proficiency means, in specific, observable language. |
| 42–45 min | Preview: the real scoring calibration comes after students take the common assessment — this is the shared picture you'll calibrate against. |

### Why This Meeting Matters

Teams often think they agree on a standard until they try to describe exactly what proficient work looks like. This meeting builds that shared picture before any assessment is written — so the common assessment is designed around one agreed definition of proficiency, not five.

You can't truly calibrate scoring until students have produced work, so that comes later, after the first common assessment. What you can do now is define proficiency in concrete terms: each teacher writes a proficient and a not-yet anchor for a key target, the team compares them, surfaces the disagreements, and refines the success criteria. The disagreements are the point — that's where a shared definition actually gets built.
